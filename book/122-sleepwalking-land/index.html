From the yearly reading list

Mia Couto has crafted a surreal, engrossing tragic tale of the hopelessness of life during the fifteen year long civil war that consumed post-independence Mozambique. Couto focuses on pulling the reader into the psyche of those affected most by war--the ones not doing the killing--the women and the children. Early on, the narrator relates one of the most bizarre stories I've ever read: his younger brother is forced to live in the family's henhouse by their insane father, and forced to act like a chicken, for so long that he eventually becomes one and disappears.

“Dreams are letters we send to our other, remaining lives.”
