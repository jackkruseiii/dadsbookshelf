From the yearly reading list

Early on, the young son of a barely-there, back-country, voodoo-meth-addicted mother makes the damning pronouncement that his mother "kills things," and indeed death hangs heavy in this novel. Ostensibly, this is a fairly straightforward narrative: a single mother, her two children, and her junkie friend embark on a road trip to pick up her boyfriend when he gets out of jail. The story's depth emerges as Ward reveals that three of the characters can see wandering dead ghosts looking for answers, and she's created characters with whom she so deeply empathizes that their visions seem real and necessary rather than fantastical.

“It feels good to be mean, to speak past the baby I can't hit.”
