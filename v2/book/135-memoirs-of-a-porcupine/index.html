This is a difficult novel from renowned author Alain Mabanckou. Hailing from the Republic of Congo, Mabanckou has penned a pointed tale aimed at taking down the role of backwoods superstition amidst modernity--at least that's what really smart people say he was doing. I will admit I didn't really understand the point of this novel and had to do some research to come to that conclusion. I was pretty disappointed I hadn't picked a different Congolese novel, but I later discovered the book won the 2006 Renaudot prize, so I've decided to give it a second chance, at least on an intellectual level.

“I cling to my memories as the elephant clings to his tusks.”
