From the shelf

Salter's wife put together a collection of his essays and articles and entitled it 'Don't Save Anything'. The title comes from Salter's long time advice 'don't save anything' — meaning don't reserve any writing ideas or details for a later piece, rather pour everything you have into each piece. It's a fitting title representing some of the best writing from Salter, the authors' author. In particular, I was struck by the Salter's terrific profile pieces in the book — I've never read such masterful writing in this genre. Reading them made me wish he'd done biographies.

“In the end, writing is like a prison, an island from which you will never be released but which is a kind of paradise: the solitude, the thoughts, the incredible joy of putting into words the essence of what you for the moment understand and with your whole heart want to believe.”

“You cannot teach someone to write any more than you can teach them to be interesting.”
